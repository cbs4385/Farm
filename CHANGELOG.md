# Changelog

## 0.6.7 (2026-10-14)

From the playtesters' list of the same day (fourth list).

- **Mining clicks reach the ground.** Only the item slots of the bar take the mouse now; a click on the bar's edge or between slots goes to the world behind it instead of doing nothing.
- **Fishing no longer covers the item bar.** The fishing panel sits above the bar. It always shows what is happening (waiting, a bite, reel it in), the bar and, when the backpack has no empty slot, a warning that a catch that does not stack will be lost. The sentences that explain the controls can be put away and brought back with the tab-back key (Q, or the left shoulder button); the choice is saved.
- **Help answers the common questions.** New topics: Energy and health, A full backpack, Cooking, Mining, Fishing, Monsters; the health topic now says how to recover. The Help tab has a search box.
- **Tips when you first meet something.** Once each: when health is low, when the backpack is full, the first time in the mine, and when a bat comes near. They stay on screen longer and wrap to fit. "Tutorial tips" in the options turns them off.
- **Monsters show when to hit them.** A monster that has just struck turns pale blue (slow to strike again: the time to swing) and turns yellow when it is about to strike; it still flashes when hit.

## 0.6.6 (2026-10-14)

From the playtesters' list of the same day (third list).

- **Ripe crops twinkle.** A small star glints over every crop and fruit tree that is ready to harvest, so a field shows what to pick without pointing at each plant. (With "Reduce flashes" on, the star is steady.)
- **The Track button is a switch.** Each quest in the Journal (and each hall room) has "Tracking: on/off"; every active quest is listed on screen unless you turn it off, and several can be on at once.
- **Menus with a pad.** The shoulder buttons change the menu tab and the cursor goes to that tab; walking left and right along the tab row shows each page, and coming up from a page no longer jumps to another tab. The right stick scrolls the open list or page (the Gazette, Gossip, Collections pages now scroll too). The crafting list follows the cursor down to the recipes you can make.
- **The mouse wheel in Options** no longer stops partway down: the list was pulled back to the control last clicked.
- **Crafting uses what is near.** Ingredients come from the backpack first, then chests within five tiles (nearest first), then finished goods waiting in machines. The recipe list counts them too.
- **Every counter gives everything its shop offers.** The shop window has a button for upgrades and services, and the upgrades window one back to the shop, from any counter (the blacksmith's, the clinic's, the saloon's bar and the rest).
- **Quality marks on pictures.** The diamond shows on a stack being dragged in the backpack and chests, on the item carried in the arms, and on a product floating over an animal.

From the playtesters' list of the same day (second list).

- **Books go back to the library.** Use the library desk with a borrowed book in the backpack and Ione takes it back, with a small gain in friendship (once for each book).

Doors join the rooms and furniture cannot be put in a doorway. Games already begun keep any furniture they moved.

- **Felled trees drop more acorns** (one most of the time, two a quarter of the time), so a planted wood more than replaces itself. The forest still grows a tree back each day.

## 0.6.2 (2026-10-13)

From the playtesters' list of the same day. The plan is in `docs/PLAYTEST_2026-10-13_PLAN.md`.

**The festivals have things to do**
- **Every festival has activities, props and a village that takes part.** From ten in the morning every villager is on the festival ground (and goes home at nine), and the props stand from dawn.
  - **Blossom Day:** a dance floor on the road, a maypole with ribbons, a band stand and bunting. The villagers by the maypole dance (a new four-frame dance for the farmer and every villager). Use the maypole to dance with the villager nearest you: it takes eight seconds, restores energy and makes a friend (once a year). Push the stick to stop.
  - **Tidewatch:** the scoreboard booth by the village pond, rowboats and a crate of fish. Enter at the board, fish in the village pond until five (a fish scores what it sells for, more for silver and gold), and come back for the results and a prize: 600 gold and a trophy for first, then 300, 150 and 50.
  - **Harvest Fair:** stalls, pumpkins and the judging table by the clock tower. Hold a crop, a preserve or a dish and use the table; the judges decide at five (600 gold and a ribbon for the best).
  - **Lantern Feast:** lantern poles, a long laid table and a lantern rack. Hang your lantern and make a wish; from five, sit down at the table to eat (full energy).
- Each prize is once a year. The villagers cast lines at the Tidewatch and wave at the fair and the feast.

**Farming and fishing**
- **The weeds of the farm have purple flowers.** 0.6.1 recolored the ground flowers but not the weed that grows in clutter, which still had orange flowers and looked like a dandelion (and a picked dandelion, growing back, was drawn as that weed). Only a dandelion is orange now.
- **A seed cannot be planted out of season** outside the greenhouse. The message names the crop and the seasons it grows in. The greenhouse is unchanged: any crop, any season.
- **The special fish do come at night.** Seven new fish bite after eight in the evening, at least two for every season at the sea and at the ponds, and they are common: Lanternfish, Cuttlefish, Stingray, Midnight Carp, Ghost Perch, Black Bass and Starfin.

**The mine and gems**
- **Gem clusters in the deeper floors** (the crystal of the Caves and Mines pack, recoloured): amethyst from floor 16, topaz 20, aquamarine 24, emerald 28, ruby 32, diamond 36. Harder gems need a better pickaxe.
- **Gems go into the Community Hall:** the boiler room asks for an amethyst, the vault for an emerald and a ruby.
- **Jewelry:** a ring (gold bar and a gem) and a pendant (iron bar and a gem) of every gem, by hand in the crafting menu (mining levels 5 and 3). They sell for much more than the gem and are always a liked gift.
- **The mine's markers are the entrance of the world map:** the way out is the entrance with a light blue inside, the stairs down are dark with an arrow down, the stairs up are lighter with an arrow up, and the lift is grey with both arrows.

**Rooms and shops**
- **Bigger furniture:** the clinic's beds are single beds two cells long, the stores' shelves are tall, the library and the hall have tall bookshelves, the big pieces of furniture you buy are drawn larger, and the farmhouse's stove is a range two cells wide and two tall.
- **The saloon sells hot meals** (every dish, at the bar).

**Bug reports, selling, and a hunt for a bug**
- **A bug report needs no words.** The subject and the description can be left empty ("Bug report" and "(nothing written)" are used); the done screen shows the full path of the file, with a button that copies the path and one that opens the folder.
- **The general store's counter has a "Sell your things to the shop" button**, so selling to Tilda is not hidden in her conversation menu (a tester could not find how the shop buys). The sell window opens over the counter and the counter comes back.
- **The log now says who changes the selected item** (a number key, the scroll wheel, a pad button, a click on the bar, a row button), to find out why an item can change by itself while watering.

**Screens**
- **A quest that is handed over shows what you carry** in the Journal and in the tracker on the screen, as the hall board already did.
- **The shipping window outlines every stack in the lot** and makes the total big and bold, so it is plain that several kinds can go at once.

## 0.6.0 (2026-10-13)

A larger update, from the playtesters' notes of the night before: how a game begins, how seeds and wild plants behave, and the library.

**Farming**
- **Every seed is sold in every season.** Planting one out of season gives a warning: it will not grow and is dead by the next morning.
- **A seedling that has not grown yet can be pulled up with the scythe the same day, and the seed comes back**, so a wrong planting costs nothing.
- **Bare tilled soil can be turned back into grass with the scythe.** (The hoe now tells you so when you swing it at tilled soil.)
- **Wild plants grow back.** Dandelions, wild garlic, berries, elderflower, hazelnuts, mushrooms, snowdrops and winter roots leave a sprout when picked, and are back after four days. Shells, clams, pearls and truffles are finds, and do not.

**Food and fighting**
- **Jam and pickles can be eaten** (they restore energy) as well as sold and given.
- **A tired farmer can still swing the sword.** A swing costs a little energy while there is any, and is never refused when there is none.

**Fixes and improvements added to this version**
- **The tool rack stands in front of the farmhouse, east of the door, with its own picture**, and the first tip tells you to take your tools from it (the tips used to say "pick the hoe" before you had one).
- **Trees overlap in the right order.** A tree lower on the screen is drawn over the one behind it, so trunks and crowns no longer cross each other.
- **Any Community Hall room can be followed from the board.** Each room has a Follow button; before, the quest the screen followed could only be changed in the Journal.
- **"Reduce flashes" now also steadies the flickering lights** (torches, lamps, glowing windows, the hearth), and the torch's flicker is gentler for everyone. The option is now called "Reduce flashes and flicker".
- **Feed the village cat.** Hold a fish or a tin of cat food (new, 40 gold at the general store) out to the cat: a tin counts as two pettings and a fish as four, up to four pettings' worth of food a day.
- **A hint the first time you walk into a shop:** click on the counter to buy and to sell.
- **A quest that waits on a villager comes before a gift.** Clicking them with an item in your hand talks to them, so you hand in the quest and do not give away the rock you had selected.
- **The opening letter says 600 gold**, which is what you have.
- **The quest list at the top right is easier to read** over grass (bold, with a dark edge).
- **The yellow flowers in the grass are pink now**, so they are not mistaken for the dandelions that quests ask for.
- **The tutorial is a tree in the Help tab.** Open the branches you want: tools, everyday things, the controls, the parts of the screen and each tab of the menu. The long walkthrough no longer starts by itself (a note points you to Help), and it can still be taken from the Help tab or the pause menu. Its Next and Back buttons stay in one place.
- **The mine keeps its floors while you are in it.** Going back up the stairs finds the floor as you left it: the monsters you killed stay dead and the ore you broke stays gone. When you leave the mine, or are knocked out, or the day ends, the next trip starts every floor new.
- **Nothing is put in the water.** Trees, rocks and plants no longer appear in the farm's pond, and ones left there by an earlier version are cleared when the map loads.
- **A click on a villager talks to them even with the fishing rod in your hand.**
- **The boiler room says what it needs.** The hall board shows how many of each thing you carry, and says that ore and coal must be raw (bars do not count).
- **The words in the bubbles over villagers' heads are twice the size.**
- **The farmer thinks aloud what to do while the first crops grow:** once the seeds are in and watered, a bubble over their head says it is time to go and see what the village has to offer.
- **Buttons of the small windows stay put:** the Yes/No, Close and Continue buttons of messages, questions, letters, the hall board, the elevator and upgrades are always in the same place at the foot of the window.
- **The tool rack is a chest:** open it to take the tools you want and put back the ones you do not.
- **The clinic, the general store and the Community Hall have new pictures** (a red cross, a sign that says GENERAL STORE, a town hall with a flag and a balcony), with their doors where the pictures show them.

**Sprinklers**
- **A sprinkler waters every tile next to it, the diagonals too** (it used to skip them). Sprinklers now cover 8, 24 and 48 tiles, and the soil is already wet when you wake.

**The farm and the village**
- **The farm's hay, sacks, wheelbarrow and barrels are drawn half as big again**, like the other things you carry, and keep that size when you set them down again.
- **New games begin with the tools on a rack at the west side of the farmhouse** and the seeds, chest and torch in the backpack. Interact with the rack to take the tools.
- **In rain and storms villagers go home after work** instead of standing about in the village square.
- **The library shelves list their books.** Look over a shelf to see some of its titles; which ones change from day to day. As the Elder God stirs, more of the titles turn strange, and then worse (at the horror setting you have chosen; with horror off the shelves stay ordinary).

- **A burning torch stays in its slot** with the bar over it, and is used up when its hour is over (it was used up as it lit).
- **A building can no longer be put down on top of you.** The mallet refuses with a notice, and a saved game that already has the player inside a building moves them out to open ground.

- **Fishing without bait says what bait would do.**

- **A bar over the torch's slot on the item bar shows how much of it is left.** A notice tells you when a torch is lit and when it burns out.

## 0.5.3 (2026-10-12)

**Light**
- **The world follows the day, between a tenth of the light and full.** It brightens through the dawn (06:00 to 09:00), is full from 09:00 to 17:00, dims through the dusk (17:00 to 21:30) and is dark through the night. This holds for every outdoor map.
- **The village lamps burn from dusk to dawn.** Each street lamp glows while the daylight is low.
- **The mine is dark: a tenth of the light, tinted blue.** You need your torch to see.
- **Everything in the world follows the light.** Monsters, the mine's stairs, villagers, animals, the farm's buildings, the grass tufts and flowers, held items and shadows used to ignore it and glowed at night; now they dim with the rest.

**Tools and friends**
- **The building mallet takes furniture, decorations, sprinklers and scarecrows into your backpack** when you swing it at them, so you can set them down anywhere, even on another map. If your backpack is full they stay where they are. Chests and machines are still carried, because they hold things. Buildings and the room's own fixtures are also carried as before.
- **Gifts reach a villager behind a shop counter.** You could not give Tilda a present while she worked at her counter; now a gift goes to whoever is under the pointer, or behind the counter in front of you.

**Fishing**
- **Bait now matters in the game.** A baited hook says so while you wait, the bite comes sooner, the green zone is wider and the rarer fish are likelier. Bait is used up when you cast.

Villager goals, remembered choices and villagers talking to each other (details in `docs/narrative/GOALS.md`).

## 0.4.5 (2026-10-11)

- **Crafting has its own key: C.** It opens the menu on the crafting page. The tool's spare key (it used to be C) is now X, matching the controller's X button; clicking still works as before.
- **The trees in the forest can be chopped.** The trees of the forest thickets used to be scenery. Now you can fell them with the axe for wood and they leave a stump, as on the farm; a felled tree stays felled. The band of trees round the edge of the map is still scenery.
- **The village green is not for chopping, breaking or digging.** The trees on the village map can be felled too (use your tools anywhere), but the villagers mind. Anyone who sees it mutters to themselves about it, and one of them walks over to tell you off for damaging public property (once a day). The same goes for breaking the rocks scattered on the green and for digging it with the hoe: the villagers comment on it, and by the next morning they have put the trees, the stones and the lawn back as they were.
- **Crafted machines are bigger.** The furnace, keg, preserves jar and the scarecrow are drawn half as big again (they still take one cell), like the chest.
- **Clicking a villager.** The mouse now picks a villager by their picture (their head counts, not only their feet) and from up to two cells away, so you can talk to someone behind a counter from the front, not only from the side.
- **Machines say what they need.** Using a furnace, keg or jar with nothing in hand lists what it takes ("Copper Bar: 5 Copper Ore and 1 Coal..."); holding the wrong thing says it cannot use it, and holding the right thing without enough says exactly what you are short of.
- **Chests with a controller.** After you move a whole stack the cursor stays on the same slot instead of jumping back to the top.
- **No mouse pointer with a controller.** The pointer is hidden and ignored (nothing under it is hovered or clicked) while a controller is in use, and comes back when you move the mouse or press a key. A notice tells you which one the game is using when a game starts and whenever it changes.
