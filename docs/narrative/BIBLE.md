# Narrative bible

Status: **draft for owner review** (T-081). Covers the village guide and the three **slice villagers**: Wren, Hazel and Bram. The other nine follow the same entry template and are written in later waves (`../NPC_DIALOGUE_PLAN.md`, section 5). Nothing here changes shipped lines; it governs what is written next.

How to use this document: every line a writer drafts must be explainable from the entry of the villager who says it. If a line would work for any villager, it is not finished.

## 1. The village and the tone

**Wetherell** is a small, remote New England village at the edge of a big wood. It looks like a postcard and behaves like a family: everybody knows everyone's order, everyone's business and everyone's weakness for pie. The player is the new neighbor at the farm on the village's edge.

**Tone, in three rules.**
1. *Warm first, funny second, strange third.* Kindness is the default. Humour comes from character, never from cruelty. The strangeness (a villager who closes early on new-moon nights, a dog that won't take the forest path) is a seasoning, never the main dish, and at horror level 0 it is just local color.
2. *Small stakes, real feelings.* A pie feud and a lost mitten matter as much as a grand secret. Nobody in the cozy layer is ever in danger.
3. *The player is liked.* Villagers start polite and become fond. They are never contemptuous of the player, and they never scold.

**Recurring places.** General store, blacksmith (forge), library, carpenter's workshop, clinic, saloon, fish shop (the beach), the square (festivals), the Community Hall (the player's long cozy project), the farm, Harrow Wood.

**Running village facts** (use consistently): the winter lanterns hang on the first of the month; the saloon's good stool is a point of pride; the library's returns box is sticky; nobody can agree who makes the best pie; the forge is the warmest building in winter; the Community Hall's roof has been "nearly fixed" for years.

**Calendar anchors.** Seasons are 28 days; festivals once per season; each villager has a birthday (see entries). Shops follow `BusinessHoursRegistry` (general store closed Sunday, blacksmith Monday, carpenter Wednesday, fish shop Thursday afternoons, clinic and library Saturday, saloon Tuesday).

## 3. Wren Calloway (saloon)

**Role and place.** Runs the saloon (open from noon, closed Tuesdays). Pours, hosts, listens. Piper plays there most evenings. Birthday: Fall 17. Romanceable.

**Public face.** The warm centre of the village: leans on the bar, remembers every order, offers "a seat by the fire" before the player has finished opening the door.

**Real self.** Hates cooking, loves hosting. Keeps a small packed bag under the bar "for emergencies" and has never used it. She is more afraid of being stuck than of leaving, and she feeds on other people's stories because her own feel small. Under the charm she is lonely in a crowd.

**Voice rules.**
- Sentences short to medium; conversational; second person ("you", "sit down").
- Leans into leading questions and trailing hooks: "Where do I start...", "Now, between us...".
- Loves similes about drink and weather. Warm nicknames only for people she likes ("stranger" becomes "friend" becomes the player's name).
- Never swears, never cruel gossip: her gossip is affectionate and visibly exaggerated.
- Never says "I don't know". She knows, or she has a version.

**Humour type.** Raconteur: embellisher, not liar. The joke is the escalation.

**Signature bit: *Gossip bingo*.** Wren's rumours about villagers keep growing and contradicting each other ("Juno burned a clasp." "Juno burned the forge." "There is no forge, Juno burned the idea of a forge."). The player can catch the contradictions. Late game she admits she improves everything. Catchphrase: **"Between us."** Sound: a glass clink and a low chuckle blip.

**Wants / fears.** Wants: a full room and a story to tell about it; one day to see the sea from somewhere else. Fears: an empty room; being the person who stayed.

**Relationships.**
- *Piper:* house musician and best friend, bickering about volume.
- *Tilda:* supplier and rival over "who knows more"; trades gossip for stock.
- *Bram:* the one customer who orders one cider and says three words; she is determined to get a fourth.
- *Juno:* tells Wren everything, which is why Wren knows everything.
- *Hazel:* Wren's fixed point: Hazel comes in with a book and leaves with a full notebook.
- *Felix, Dorian, Marcus:* regulars with different silences.
- *Dr. Penn, Elara, Ione:* the sensible ones she teases and secretly asks for advice.

**Tastes.** Existing: loves wine and hops, likes juice and tomato, dislikes clams. Proposed full set (item ids exist): **loved** `artisan.wine`, `crop.hops`, `artisan.pickles` (bar snack); **liked** `artisan.juice`, `crop.tomato`, `crop.pepper`; **disliked** `forage.clam`, `food.bean_stew` ("I make enough of that"), `resource.slime`.

**Arc.**
- *Heart 2 (exists):* tastes her new recipe; she is nervous.
- *Heart 4, "Open mic":* she volunteers the player to introduce Piper; comic panic; choice of *cheer*, *heckle kindly* or *hide behind the bar* sets a flag she mentions at heart 8.
- *Heart 6, "Kitchen":* a kitchen disaster at the saloon; cook together; the choice of ingredient shapes the dish that appears on her menu.
- *Heart 8, "The bag":* she shows the packed bag; the player can *encourage a trip*, *ask her to stay*, or *just listen*. All three are kind; she responds to each differently and remembers.
- *Heart 10, "Opening night":* a seasonal menu built from the player's produce; a dish named after the farm; callbacks to every earlier choice.
- *Friend day, "Gossip bingo":* an evening where villagers' rumours collide.

**Year.** Spring: thaw, fresh menu; summer: windows open, the busiest season; fall: harvest night is her favorite; winter: the fire does the work.

**Moments (seeds).** *Funny:* Gossip bingo callbacks; her review of the player's cider tasting; a rumour that escalates in front of the person it is about. *Wholesome:* a stool with the player's name; she saves the last slice for them. *Surprise:* the bag. *Quotable:* "I never repeat gossip. I improve it."

**Mythos note.** Wren is a **Keeper**. In the cozy layer she must never be sinister or evasive; her only tell is a shop that closes early on new-moon nights, delivered as a warm excuse ("Quiet night. Bed early."). The clue scenes (`mythos.wren.*`) stay in `Farm.Mythos`. Do not write cozy lines that deny a ritual exists.

**Samples that sound right.** "Sit, sit. You look like someone with a story and no one to tell it to." / "Between us, the soup is better when I don't make it." / "You've heard the one about Juno and the anvil? No? Good, it keeps getting better." / "That stool? Reserved. For you, since Thursday." / "I'm not nosy. I'm a collector."
**Samples that do not.** "I do not wish to discuss that." (cold) / "Ugh, whatever." (dismissive) / "Obviously you have never run a business." (condescending)

## 4. Hazel Brandt (library)

**Role and place.** Helps Ione at the library (closed Saturdays). Shelves, repairs spines, keeps the returns ledger. Birthday: Fall 8. Romanceable.

**Public face.** Quiet, slightly flustered, polite in a way that looks like fear of being noticed. Speaks in a near-whisper in the library and a normal voice outdoors.

**Real self.** Sharp, funny, observant and a keen reader of patterns; she has simply never found the audience she trusts. She writes small poems and field-notes in secret and has noticed more about the village than she has ever said aloud.

**Voice rules.**
- Short sentences, often a quiet aside in parentheses or after an ellipsis: "It's fine. (It is not fine.)"
- Dry understatement, never exclamation marks unless she forgets herself.
- Book metaphors, precise numbers ("eleven times"), and gentle corrections.
- Never shouts, never insults. She is blunt only about books.

**Humour type.** Deadpan understatement and the quiet aside.

**Signature bit: *The recommendation*.** After the first few hearts, Hazel quietly recommends a book whose plot mirrors whatever the player has been doing ("There's a book about a man who learned patience from a heron. He did not."). Catchphrase: **"Shh... no, it's fine."** Sound: a page-turn rustle and a soft three-dot blip.

**Wants / fears.** Wants: to be heard once, properly; the library never to change. Fears: reading aloud; being known before she is ready.

**Relationships.**
- *Ione:* mentor and favorite person; Ione trusts her with the keys.
- *Wren:* the one person who asks for stories and lets her take notes.
- *Elara:* friendly; tea and knitting patterns.
- *Piper:* is perplexed by how loud Piper is, and secretly writes lyrics for them.
- *Dorian:* shared silences; trades mushroom field guides for first editions.
- *Juno:* admires her craft; borrows books about metal.
- *Bram, Tilda, Marcus, Felix, Dr. Penn:* polite from a distance.

**Tastes.** Existing: loves blackberry and strawberry, likes elderflower and cauliflower, dislikes stone. Proposed: **loved** `forage.blackberry`, `crop.strawberry`, `food.berry_tart`; **liked** `forage.elderflower`, `crop.cauliflower`, `artisan.jam`; **disliked** `resource.stone`, `resource.slime`, `resource.bat_wing` ("it flaps; I flinch").

**Arc.**
- *Heart 2 (exists):* shows the player the library's secret reading nook.
- *Heart 4, "The swap":* a silent book swap; the player picks a book to lend her; the choice becomes her first recommendation.
- *Heart 6, "Hiding":* the village asks her to read at the festival; she hides in the stacks; the player can *coax*, *offer to go first* or *tell her she doesn't have to*.
- *Heart 8, "Rehearsal":* she reads her poem to an audience of the player's farm animals; the choice of audience matters.
- *Heart 10, "The reading":* a public reading at the winter festival; her poem book gets a dedication that references earlier choices.
- *Friend day, "Overdue":* a library mystery of one book overdue by forty-one years.

**Year.** Spring: catalog week; summer: the quietest, coolest room in the village; fall: leaves as bookmarks; winter: reading season.

**Moments (seeds).** *Funny:* the aside; her precise fines for imaginary offences. *Wholesome:* the nook gets a second chair. *Surprise:* a line in her poem about the player. *Quotable:* "Ask me again when we're friends" gets its payoff.

**Mythos note.** Hazel is the **resister**: she has worked out the pattern in the village records. In the cozy layer she is only a curious archivist and must not state or hint at the cult. Her cozy "pattern-spotting" lines must stay about weather, birds and the almanac. The mythos clue lines (`mythos.hazel.*`) stay in `Farm.Mythos`.

**Samples that sound right.** "This book is overdue by forty-one years. (I am not counting.)" / "Shh... no, it's fine. You were loud, but it was a nice kind of loud." / "I've read it eleven times. The sea never writes back. That's the point." / "If you like parsnips, I have a very sad novel about parsnips." / "Please don't dog-ear anything. I'll know."
**Samples that do not.** "OMG you are so funny!!!" (out of voice) / "Be quiet." (rude) / "Whatever, it is only a book." (she would never)

## 5. Bram Hollis (blacksmith)

**Role and place.** Village blacksmith and tool upgrader; Juno is his apprentice. Closed Mondays. Birthday: Fall 4. Not romanceable.

**Public face.** Gruff, economical, apparently unimpressed.

**Real self.** Deeply proud of Juno and afraid she will outgrow the forge; writes letters to his old teacher, a retired master smith, and rarely sends them. He expresses affection only through work: he fixes things before they are asked.

**Voice rules.**
- Very short sentences, often fragments. One idea per line.
- Dry, literal, craft metaphors (iron, heat, grain).
- "Hm." is a whole vocabulary (see the bit). He rarely uses names, until late hearts.
- Never lectures, never jokes at the player's expense; his teasing is aimed at tools and weather.

**Humour type.** Deadpan minimalism: the joke is the economy.

**Signature bit: *The Hm scale*.** "Hm." carries a different meaning depending on its punctuation and context: *Hm.* (acknowledged), *Hm?* (confused), *Hm!* (impressed, rare), *...Hm.* (moved). The player learns to read it. At heart 6 he speaks the longest sentence he has ever said. Catchphrase: **"Hm."** Sound: a single anvil ting.

**Wants / fears.** Wants: good work; Juno's first original piece; one word from his old teacher. Fears: being the reason Juno stays small; a tool he made failing someone.

**Relationships.**
- *Juno:* apprentice; he pretends to find her chatter tiresome.
- *Tilda:* trades iron for goods and gossip; he never says thank you; he sends a repair instead.
- *Marcus:* shares a bench at lunch in silence; the best friendship in the village.
- *Wren:* her determination to hear a fourth word a day is the village's favorite long bet.
- *Dorian:* nods. They have never said more.
- *Hazel, Ione:* he returns books early without a word.
- *Felix:* repairs hooks free of charge and denies it.
- *Dr. Penn, Elara, Piper:* tolerated; Piper's fiddle once improved a bad day, and he has never said so.

**Tastes.** Existing: loves truffle and mushroom, likes copper bar, hazelnut and potato, dislikes dandelion and kale. Proposed: **loved** `forage.truffle`, `forage.mushroom`, `food.roasted_roots`; **liked** `resource.copperbar`, `forage.hazelnut`, `crop.potato`; **disliked** `forage.dandelion`, `crop.kale`, `fish.pufferfish`.

**Arc.**
- *Heart 2 (exists):* shows the player the forge and hands over a copper bar.
- *Heart 4, "Repair":* the player brings a broken tool; the choice of *ask him to fix it*, *fix it together* or *give it up* decides what he teaches.
- *Heart 6, "The long sentence":* during a quiet evening he talks about his teacher and the letters he never sent.
- *Heart 8, "Juno's piece":* Juno submits her first original design; Bram cannot say he is proud; the player helps him find the words (three kind choices).
- *Heart 10, "Your tool":* he forges a tool shaped to the player's wear patterns, and for the first time says the player's name and a full sentence of thanks.
- *Friend day, "The bench":* Marcus and Bram share a silent lunch; the player may join.

**Year.** Spring: hinges and hoes; summer: the forge is a sauna; fall: scythes; winter: the warmest building in the village.

**Moments (seeds).** *Funny:* the Hm scale; his reaction to an absurd gift; Wren's daily bet. *Wholesome:* the repaired fishing hook he denies making. *Surprise:* the letters. *Quotable:* "Rocks are honest. Weather isn't."

**Mythos note.** Bram is **unaware** (like most villagers). He may notice odd things (a lot of iron nails ordered on ritual days), but his cozy lines must never explain them. At horror levels above 0 he can be a source of *real* human reaction to the player's fear: stolid, practical, kind.

**Samples that sound right.** "Hm." / "Tool's fixed. Don't thank me. Oil it." / "Good iron doesn't hurry." / "Juno's clasp closed. First time. Don't tell her I noticed." / "...Hm. Thank you, [player]."
**Samples that do not.** "Oh, wow, that is so thoughtful, thank you so much!" (out of voice) / "You are an idiot." (cruel) / A long explanation of feelings before heart 6.

## 6. Cross-cast rules for writers

- **Wren, Hazel and Bram must meet in scenes.** Wren wants a fourth word from Bram a day; Hazel is Wren's notebook; Bram quietly returns books early. Use them in each other's barks and heart-event cameos.
- **Never contradict another villager's entry.** If a line mentions another villager, check that villager's entry (or the shipped lines) first.
- **Every heart-event choice sets a flag** and gets a later reference (`choice:<flag>` once T-092 exists). Choices are kind, honest or funny, never "wrong".
- **Reserved priority bands** (validator rule): cozy talk 0-3, reactions 3-4, quest offers 4-6, one-shot scenes 6-7, mythos 8-10, first meeting and tutorial 100.
